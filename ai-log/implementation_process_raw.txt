  History: 20 20 20 300 20 20 20 20

  Example 3: why the 20% guard exists (B6/B7)
  History: 200 × 8. The MAD is 0, so the √200 floor gives a spread of 14.1 and a

                                      235.4

                                          236   241

  For a large account, a band based only on noise is tight, so the 20% guard
  stops small but statistically "significant" changes from raising a flag.

  Example 4: a usual of zero (B3/B4)
  History: 0 × 8, so usual is 0, the band is [0, 0] and there's no percentage.

     week = 2 → outside the band, but only 2 events (< 3)  → ● NORMAL

  shown as "—")

  Example 5: when the MAD sets the spread (a volatile account)
  History: 100 100 100 100 120 120 120 120. The median is 110 and the MAD is 10,
  so 1.4826 × 10 = 14.8. That's larger than √110 = 10.5, so the spread is 14.8
  and the band is 72.9 – 147.1.

  This account's normal wobble is bigger than chance alone, so the band widens
  to match. A quiet, steady account gets a tighter band.

  Example 6: low volume (B5)
  History: 5 × 8. The spread is √5 = 2.24, so the lower bound is 5 − 5.6, which
  is clamped to 0. A week of 0 is not below 0, so the result is ● NORMAL, with
  low_volume = true. At this volume even a drop to zero can't be told apart from
